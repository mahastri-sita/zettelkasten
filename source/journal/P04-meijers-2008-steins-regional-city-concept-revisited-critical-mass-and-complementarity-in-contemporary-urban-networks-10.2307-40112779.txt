      Much claimed, littleproven

      Obviously,thereis a difference  in termsof genesisbetweentheconceptof theurban
      networkand Stein's Regional City.While a clusteringof citiesoftenevolved either
      froma historyof fragmented     politicaland administrativestructuresas well as rivalry
      (e.g. Randstad), or from  rapid urbanisation  during the IndustrialEra followingthe
      discovery of natural resources  as ore and  coal (Ruhr Area), thispolycentricpattern
      gained significancemore recentlyas relativedistancesbetweenthemdecreased and
      functionalrelationships startedto increase.Conversely, accordingto Stein,a Regional
      City  would result from a  deliberate integralplanningdesign. However,in termsof
      spatial structure characteristics,both  concepts hold the same ideal configurations.
      Stein makes two main assumptionsin thisrespect.

      (1) The mass or supportpresentin thepolycentric Regional Cityis equivalentto those
          generatedbymonocentriccities,whichwould resultin a similarpresenceof urban
          facilitiesand amenities.
      (2) Facilitiesand amenitiesare dispersedacrosstheconstituentcitiesor townsin such
          a way thateach one of themhostssome of them,but not the same, thusmaking
          thecitiesor townscomplementone another.

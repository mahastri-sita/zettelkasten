    ABSTRACT
    Although there seems to be a widely shared expectation that polycentricity increases economic competitiveness, the
    empirical ﬁndings are inconsistent. As the ﬁrst meta-analysis, this paper examines whether emerging empirical
    evidence provides an insight into the polycentricity–productivity effect. Based on 139 estimates, we ﬁnd no
    compelling evidence to support that polycentricity is either beneﬁcial or detrimental for economic development.
    Further meta-logit regressions show that the measurement of polycentricity and the control of agglomeration
    economies indeed affect the polycentricity–productivity effect. In contrast, measuring polycentricity from different
    perspectives and the potential endogeneity do not appear to signiﬁcantly inﬂuence this effect.
    KEYWORDS
    spatial structure; polycentricity; economic performance; meta-analysis

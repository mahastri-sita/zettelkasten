    ABSTRACT
    Research examining the economic effects of urban polycentricity remains inconclusive. We contribute to this debate by
    developing a longitudinal framework in which changes in polycentricity in Chinese urban regions are linked with
    changes in total factor productivity. While we ﬁnd no evidence of urban polycentricity being conducive to economic
    growth, we observe that the relationship depends on population size and the interactions between cities. We also ﬁnd
    that cities borrow size from nearby cities in large urban regions, contributing to regional economic growth. We use
    our ﬁndings to reﬂect on China’s regional economic and urban development strategies.
    KEYWORDS
    polycentricity; urban regions; productivity; borrowed size; China
